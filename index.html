McLaren: a short history

A quick timeline of Bruce McLaren, the racing team, and the road-car era.

1937

Bruce McLaren is born

New Zealand racing driver and engineer Bruce McLaren was born in Auckland on 30 August 1937.

1963

Team founded

Bruce McLaren founded Bruce McLaren Motor Racing Ltd. in 1963, building a team around racing engineering and competition.

1966

Formula 1 debut

The McLaren Formula 1 team made its World Championship debut at the Monaco Grand Prix in 1966.

1968

First F1 victory

Bruce McLaren won the 1968 Belgian Grand Prix in a McLaren-Ford, giving the team its first Formula 1 World Championship Grand Prix win.

1970

Bruce McLaren dies

Bruce McLaren died in a testing accident at Goodwood on 2 June 1970, aged 32. The team continued under his name.

1981

Carbon-fibre monocoque

McLaren’s MP4/1 introduced a carbon-fibre composite monocoque to Formula 1, a landmark in racing-car construction.

1988

A dominant F1 season

McLaren-Honda won 15 of 16 Grands Prix in the 1988 season with Alain Prost and Ayrton Senna.

1992–1998

F1 road-car icon

The McLaren F1 road car entered production in the early 1990s. Its central driving position and lightweight construction made it a landmark supercar.

2011

Modern road-car era

McLaren Automotive launched the MP4-12C, marking the start of its modern series-production supercar range.

2013

P1 hybrid hypercar

The McLaren P1 combined a twin-turbo V8 with electric assistance, reflecting the growing role of hybrid technology in high-performance cars.

2017

720S arrives

The 720S joined the Super Series with a carbon-fibre structure and a twin-turbocharged V8.

2020s

New performance era

McLaren expanded its road-car family with models such as the Artura hybrid supercar and continued to develop lightweight performance engineering.

Historical overview for learning and entertainment. Model specifications and timelines can vary by market and production year.

About AutoAR Studio

A personal 3D garage built around your supplied McLaren model files.

3D garage

Load the bundled GLB models, rotate and zoom, use camera views, and try supported AR viewing on compatible devices.

02

Design controls

Paint controls attempt to recolor compatible materials. Results depend on how each source model separates its materials.

03

Sound Lab

The engine-style sound is synthesized locally by Web Audio. It is not a genuine recorded McLaren engine or a mechanical simulation.

04

AR requirements

AR needs HTTPS and device/browser support. Android Chrome with WebXR/Scene Viewer is usually the best route; iPhone uses Quick Look where supported.

05

Model files

Four GLB files are included: McLaren Spider, 2022 765LT, 720S LBWK unfinished model, and McLaren concept model. Names describe the uploaded files and aren't factory-authenticity claims.
